0.x gives every topic the same per-paper, four-tier, three-model treatment. That suits a narrow research question and fails a broad field, where thousands of papers are legitimately on topic. v1 gives each kind of topic the service that matches what a miss costs.

What changes for you

  • A web app is the primary surface: inbox, briefings, search, topic editor, report-a-miss, BibTeX.
  • Your labels and reported misses become the evaluation set, so recall on your question is a number you can see.
  • Notion, n8n, Zotero, Nextcloud, the PDF archive and deep cards become optional integrations.
  • Model choices are roles in one config file. Proposed defaults put triage, judging and reviews on Anthropic credits and keep one other lab as the second grader.

    Settled on 2026-10-08

    • Zotero items from broad selections stay where they are. v1 stops adding to them and deletes nothing.
    • Second grader: Muse preferred, for the third lab. Milestone 2 measures it against Luna before the default is fixed.
    • Radar picks are not saved automatically; you save the ones that matter.
    • Misses from a phone: the web app is an installable PWA that appears in Android's share sheet, so a shared arXiv link arrives as a miss report. Part of milestone 3.
    • Search is one box and one result list over litwatch and, when its integration is on, arxiv-archive. Results merge by arXiv id; a paper only the archive holds can be brought in with one action; the source is an ordinary filter.
    • Topic edits apply forward. Nothing retroactive runs by itself; you can start a re-run over a chosen scope, which shows its paper count and estimated cost.
    • Your PI's view: the Notion export carries Watch topics only, until you have talked to her.

    Risks worth knowing

    • Triage is the single point where a paper can vanish. Behind it: the keyword audit, the miss channel, and a recall number on the Quality page. Milestone 2 does not pass if triage loses papers 0.14 found, and it includes a hand check of what triage rejects.
    • DBLP access. Its HTTP search API answered this server with a bot challenge on 2026-10-08; its SPARQL endpoint worked (exact-title lookup in about 50 ms). The lookup sits behind an interface, with a local data dump as fallback.
    • An edit does not reach back. An old paper the previous wording excluded stays unfound unless a keyword or a date-range re-run reaches it. Embeddings over the archive, planned for later, would close this.
    • Cost figures are estimates from 0.x token counts and are re-measured in milestone 2.
