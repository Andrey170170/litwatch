litwatch v1: web app design

Draft for discussion, 2026-10-11, second pass. Nothing here is built.

What the web app is

It is the user side of litwatch: the place where the value the system produces is handed over. Litwatch reads five to six hundred papers a night so that one person does not have to, and the web app is how that person gets the result: what is new in their field, which papers matter for their question, and enough about each to decide whether to read it. The pipeline that produces this (triage, keyword audit, two graders, reconciliation, runs, integrations) is the kitchen. It is reachable, through Settings, but it is not on the table.

The first draft of this document framed the app as a decision queue: an Inbox, splits that "need you", labels with one keystroke. That was the spec's wording taken literally, and it is wrong. On a normal morning nothing needs the owner. They read.

How it is used

  • Every morning, usually on a phone: open Today and read it like a newspaper. First the research questions (the Watch topics): the new papers that matter, each with a short written take. Then the field: the daily review of interpretability, and on Mondays the weekly reviews. It ends, and says so.
  • When a paper looks good: open it. Abstract, litwatch's take, the deep card, links to arXiv and the PDF, save to the library, copy BibTeX. One tap each, with the pen already there.
  • When working on a topic: open its page and read down the list of what the topic has found, newest first, as the reading list for current research. Adjacent papers are there too, folded.
  • When looking for something: search. One box over litwatch's collection and the arxiv-archive together. One of the possible outcomes of a search is "the automation missed this", and that is where a miss is reported from.
  • Occasionally: glance at the status chip to see that the night went fine; thumb a paper up or down to tell the chef; open Settings to edit a topic, check quality, or look at an integration.

Navigation

Today, Topics, Library, Search, and a gear. Behind the gear: Topics (edit, preview, re-run), Quality, Misses, Runs, Integrations, Configuration. On the phone the four are a bottom bar and the gear sits in the header. Nothing from behind the gear appears in front of it.

Look

White. Serif for titles, the date, the lede and the reviews, since those are what is read; the system sans for everything that is interface. The plan document's dark green is the one accent. The only colour on the reading surface is the status chip's dot and the small saved mark. Grade colours are kept for Settings and for the quiet dots on topic lists, where the order already says what the dot repeats.

What is on the page, and what is not

  • The lede is written by the digest writer from the night's verdicts: what came in, in one breath. It is the only place counts appear outside the chip.
  • Sections are the Watch topics in the owner's order, then the Radar topics with a daily cadence, then on Mondays the weekly ones. A topic with nothing new is a single line ("Nothing new for CoT faithfulness"), never an empty section.
  • Lead entries are the papers graded must read: a two or three sentence take. Brief entries are the relevant ones: one sentence. A paper the graders disagreed on is shown at the more inclusive grade and looks like any other.
  • Adjacent papers are not on Today. The fold line says how many and sends you to the topic pages.
  • The action row is the pen: Read opens the paper page (on the desktop, the reading pane), then arXiv, PDF, Save, BibTeX. Saved is a state, not a button. The thumbs sit at the end, quiet until used.
  • Days away. The pager walks back a day at a time, and the header notes how many days are unread. Merging unread days into one page is a later improvement; the per-day pages are the data either way.
  • No "mark read". The page ends when it ends. What was opened and saved is remembered without a ritual.

How this was chosen

▲▼Tell the chef

Decisions on this page

  • Take above abstract. The take is written for the owner and is why the paper is here; the abstract is the authors' and comes second. Both are short; the deep card is the long read.
  • Deep card is present for must-read papers (the spec's deep integration requests one on that grade) and otherwise a single button, "Request a deep card", in its place. The card's sections come from the integration; the page renders what it is given.
  • BibTeX copies to the clipboard with the published version's venue when DBLP has one, otherwise the arXiv entry. Save adds to the library and is what Zotero and the other integrations react to.
  • Topic chips say which topics this paper belongs to. A paper can be in several; the take is written once, for the first topic it was graded relevant for, and the card names each topic it speaks to.
  • How this was chosen unfolds to: the triage decision, the two graders' grades and reasons by model name, the reconciled grade, and the owner's label if any. That is the content the old Inbox put first. Here it is last and closed.

Search and Library

Search is one box over everything: litwatch's collection, and the arxiv-archive through the archive integration, merged by arXiv id (spec, decision 6). It is a real tool, used because it is closer to hand than Google, and later a semantic one. Where a result comes from is a filter beside topic and year. A paper that only the archive holds is marked as outside the collection, with one action to fetch it in and, if it belonged to a Watch topic, to say so: that is how a miss is reported. The library is the saved pile and where export happens.

The other Settings pages, in words

  • Topics: the list of topics with kind, cadence, revision and status, and the editor from the first draft (subject, rubric, keywords with match counts; preview a draft against labelled papers and misses; scoped re-runs after saving; pause).
  • Quality is what the chip opens. At the top, last night: the disagreements with both opinions and the label row, so a person who wants to settle them can. Below, per Watch topic: the four recalls and precision over evaluation runs, each number a link to its papers; the correction rate from thumbs; and a "draw a calibration sample" button that puts ten random candidates on the existing labelling page for a precision figure that is not biased by what the owner chose to react to.
  • Misses: reported misses with their stage traces (the first draft's trace page), open and resolved.
  • Runs and cost: a row per night with counts, duration, cost by role and provider, errors; the month's total; papers triage is stuck on, with a dismiss.
  • Configuration: the loaded litwatch.toml as read, secrets masked, with the models in use per role and the lab rule's verdict. Read-only in v1; the file stays the source of truth, which keeps the API free of a config-write path and the token's power bounded.
  • Integration cards are the generic rendering of the Integration protocol's status: enabled, a one-line description the module supplies, cursor lag, failures with the last error, retry and skip. A module that also implements SearchProvider reports its answer rate and latency. A new integration gets a card without any frontend work.

Pipeline: the digest writer

Today needs written takes, so the pipeline gains one step after reconciliation: for each paper graded relevant or above, a writer produces the take (two or three sentences for must read, one for relevant) from the abstract, the two graders' reasons and the topic's subject, in litwatch's voice and addressed to the owner. Once a night it also writes the lede from the day's verdicts. A mid-sized model (Sonnet 5.5 as the proposal, settled by evaluation like the other roles); at one to five papers a night the cost is cents. The take is stored on the verdict and regenerated only when the verdict changes. This belongs in M3, since the page has nothing to say without it, and it is the same shape as the M4 review writer, which can share its prompt scaffolding.

Feedback model

Thumbs are stored as reactions (paper, topic, value up or down, reason, time). The reason "unrelated to the topic" also writes a label (not relevant, origin reader) and "great find" writes relevant; the other reasons do not touch labels. Opening a paper page and saving are recorded as events already (paper.saved exists; paper.opened is new and local). Quality reports the correction rate from reactions and keeps precision honest with the calibration sample rather than from reactions, which are biased by construction. The existing labelling page stays as the evaluation tool.

API

/inbox becomes /digest (days, with sections and takes). /feedback gains reactions. /integrations grows the status shape the cards render (description, lag, failures, retry, skip, search provider stats). Everything else stands.

M3, restated

M3 · API and web app, Watch first. The API with its OpenAPI schema and generated client; the writer step; Today, Paper, Topic, Library, Search and Settings over Watch data, with the field sections of Today arriving in M4. Check: the morning digest reads as prose with no grade, model or pipeline term on it; a paper can be opened, saved and exported as BibTeX in one tap each; the status chip reflects a failed run and a disagreement; a reported miss shows what each stage said, under Settings; nothing about the pipeline is visible without opening Settings.
