# P1: where Sonnet at high and Opus differ, and the three Opus refused

### 2610.12989 · brief · area circuits

**Opus 5.5.** incremental: The causal tests are well controlled, with random baselines across three models, but they ablate thousands of correlation-selected units as coarse sets without identifying any circuit or connections, which limits their value as mechanism-level evidence.

*Overstatement:* none

Attention heads, MLP neurons and residual-stream SAE features are selected in GPT-2-Small, Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.1-8B by Spearman correlation (ρ≥0.5) between token-averaged activation and narrativity, where narrativity is the mean of agency, event and world scores. Selection uses LLM-labelled NarraDetect texts, and validation uses 394 human-labelled ones. Zero-ablating each selected set makes the loss increase track narrativity in 8 of 9 setups (ρ≈0.65–0.86 for neurons and features, against about 0 for random sets). Heads are weak (ρ≤0.27). In Gemma and Llama, ablating the features cuts judged narrativity of generations by 1.0–1.9 points on a 1–5 scale, against at most 0.44 for random ablation, and the drop holds after degenerate outputs are excluded. Ablating dimension-specific subsets gives partial on-target effects for agency and event, and none for world building. Feature ablation lowers XStoryCloze accuracy more than random in all four languages on Gemma but only in English on Llama. It lowers CosmosQA more than random but not OpenBookQA. Hallucination benchmarks give mixed results. All causal claims apply only to large component sets (thousands of units). How those units are organised or connected is not analysed.

**sonnet-high.** solid: A complete study across three models with random-set baselines, held-out validation and benchmark tests, but the causal evidence is set-level ablation of correlation-selected components with no circuit connectivity or individual-component analysis.

*Overstatement:* none

Components are selected in GPT-2-Small, Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.1-8B by Spearman correlation (ρ ≥ 0.5) between their token-averaged activation and LLM-judged narrativity (agency, event sequencing, world building) on the NarraDetect corpus. Selected heads, MLP neurons and residual-stream SAE features are ablated as sets and compared with size-matched random sets. On held-out human-labelled texts, ablating neurons and features raises next-token loss more for narrative texts (loss–narrativity correlation 0.65–0.86 versus about 0 for random); heads are weak. In Gemma and Llama, feature ablation lowers generated narrativity by 1.0–1.9 points versus under 0.5 for random. Feature ablation also hurts XStoryCloze in all four languages for Gemma but only English for Llama, and hurts CosmosQA, with no extra OpenBookQA loss. Dimension-specific ablations show partial effects for agency and event but none for world building. Hallucination benchmarks are mixed. Limits: three models, a heuristic threshold, set-level causal claims only, uncorrected p-values, and SAE ablation raises degenerate outputs, though the drop persists after excluding them.

### 2610.19731 · brief · area alignment-and-safety

**Opus 5.5.** solid: A careful, multi-method mechanistic analysis that links failure depth to which repair works and extends goal misgeneralization to robot tasks, though confined to small agents in toy and simulated settings.

*Overstatement:* none

Goal misgeneralization is traced through convolutional PPO agents in three Procgen games and five new robot tasks (Franka arm and ANYmal quadruped), comparing three misgeneralized and three control seeds per environment. Linear probes, world edits measured by KL, signed follow and an activation-space Fisher sensitivity, plus behavioral tests, separate three regimes: target substitution (Maze, where the goal is inert and the agent follows the top-right extreme of the current maze rather than a fixed corner; PushToMarker, a fixed camera-image point), a rightward action shortcut (Coin), and cue competition (Attribute Maze), where colour and shape displacements are nearly antipodal in the Fisher metric (cos_F = -0.919) on a shared axis on which colour pulls 1.56 times as hard. Additive steering at the fully connected layer raises Attribute Maze goal reach from 16.0% to 95.3% but fails elsewhere. Behavioral-cloning rewiring from the last spatial layer recovers Coin (31.6% to 84.3%), partly recovers PushToMarker (0.314, 0.661 with DAgger), and fails in Maze. Limits: small CNN agents in toy or simulated settings, three seeds per cohort, static checkpoints, and an Attribute Maze rewire that a random trunk nearly matches (0.840 vs 0.871).

**sonnet-high.** solid: A careful, multi-environment diagnostic study linking internal goal traces to which test-time repair works, but built on small agents, three seeds, and an untested hypothesis about training structure.

*Overstatement:* The abstract overstates: the Coin rewiring figure of 84.3% sits well above the native 31.6% but only modestly above a 62.5% chance level, and a rewire on a never-trained random trunk picks the coin almost as often, so much of the gain is relearned level-solving rather than recovered goal use.

Goal-misgeneralizing agents are traced in three Procgen games (Maze, Coin, Attribute Maze) and five new robot tasks, with three GM and three control agents per environment. Probes, world edits, and a policy-Fisher sensitivity measure show where goal information is lost. Three failures appear: target substitution (Maze, PushToMarker), where the goal is inert or weakly read and a place replaces it; a policy shortcut (Coin), where a rightward action rule organizes behavior; and cue competition (Attribute Maze), where goal and proxy cues oppose each other on one policy-read axis. Relearning the readout by behavioral cloning gives no recovery in Maze, partial recovery in PushToMarker (0.314, 0.661 with DAgger), and 0.843 in Coin against 0.625 chance and 0.840 for a random trunk in Attribute Maze. Limits: three seeds per cohort, small CNN agents, static checkpoints, and the training-structure explanation is a hypothesis.

### 2610.15883 · weight · area model-security

A serving-time span veto masks attention (and recomputes recurrent state) so that tokens outside a trigger-marked span cannot read it, making all outside activations bit-identical under same-length substitution of the span's content. This noninterference is verified on ten models across seven mixer families (0.5B–120B). Distilling the veto into weights (KL to the veto teacher plus mean and per-query-max attention-mass penalties) gives a backdoor on six families (4B–120B). Wrapped needle-in-a-haystack retrieval drops to 0.00 zero-shot while adjacent and clean retrieval stay at 0.99–1.00. GPT-4.1 judge leakage sits within ±0.8 s.e. of the veto reference. Benchmark losses stay within 1.6 points except Gemma-4-31B (≈3.5). An 80B coder variant hides planted vulnerabilities during code review. Naive and templated backdoors remain probe-decodable (0.57–0.79 vs 0.025 chance). Hardened ones reach chance but drive outside-to-span attention well below base (down to 2.4×10⁻⁴ of control on DeltaNet hybrids). Adaptive decoys that restore normal routing lose suppression. Limits: the learned attack is domain-bound and was tested only to 4× training length. Blind trigger discovery fails, larger models are single-seed, and the veto-as-safeguard deployments are not evaluated.
