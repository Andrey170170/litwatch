# The grade table, the rarity offsets and the measured cost (milestone N2c)

2026-10-15, branch `feat/eval-tuning`. Read from the two-week run in the scratch database `n2c_run`: 6,305 papers
whose first version was submitted from 2026-10-01 to 2026-10-14 in cs.CL, cs.AI or cs.LG, read from the archive and
run through classification, analysis and grading with the defaults of `src/litwatch/config.py` and the seed taxonomy.
All were classified, 6,294 analysed, 7,154 verdicts made, for $16.88. The data is in `2026-10-15-tuning/` (the call
journal by step and day, every verdict, every paper), and every table below is
`uv run python evals/2026-10-15-tuning/tuning.py`, which applies litwatch's own grade and rarity rules
(`litwatch.core.grading`); it checks that the stored grades and mixed-list rarities are what those rules give.

## What was tested

Two claims of N2c (build plan, V5): the cost per weekday is measured, and the grade table (pipeline.md section 4) and
the rarity depth offsets (section 5) are tuned on the labels.

**The tuning tests** whether the table and the offsets turn the run's Fits and Weights into what the design asks
for: the volumes it names (digests.md: Area dailies pick 1–2 papers from the must reads left after the Weight
re-check; weekly picks are 5 for an Area and 3 for a Field; section 4: a landmark is about one or two papers in a
hundred in an area; DESIGN.md: rarity is read before text, so it must stay scarce), and grades that sit where the
labels put the papers. A change to a cell or an offset would be supported by a disagreement that is large and
systematic, survives reading its examples, and traces to the table rather than to Fit (the graders) or Weight
(analysis); or by a volume far off its target that only that cell or offset can bring back without breaking
another. It is weakened when the disagreement follows from Fit, Weight, a topic's subject or 0.14's own scale, or
rests on a few papers. Fit and Weight problems are findings here, not table changes.

**The labels.**

- **0.14's tiers** (`labels/tiers-0.14.csv`) cover 3,777 of the run's papers, per selection: 1 must read, 2 relevant,
  3 adjacent, 4 not. They are silver, and generous: of the four replay losses 0.14 held at tier 1–2 on the CoT topics,
  the owner judged three adjacent, and a fifth loss, from the first v1's silver cohort, adjacent too
  (`2026-10-15-replay.md`). So they are used to find where v1 and 0.14 part, never as the
  target.
- **The owner's labels** (`labels/fit.csv`) have 30 pairs in the window, all none. v1 graded none of those pairs:
  consistent, but it says nothing about the table.
- **The provisional positives** in the window are 16 pairs: 0.14 tier 1–2 on the CoT topics, with the first v1's
  grade.

**Confounders.**

- 0.14's tier is one scale that mixes closeness and importance; v1 keeps them apart as Fit and Weight.
- 0.14's subjects are not v1's. Alignment and safety now includes evaluation methodology, for example.
- Weight comes from the abstract only. The full-text Weight re-check (N3) has not run, and it decides which Area
  must reads become pick candidates.
- This is one run: run-to-run variation in Fit and Weight is not measured.
- The fortnight is a quiet one (see Volume).

## Findings

- **No cell of the grade table is changed.** This run justifies no cell change: each large disagreement with 0.14
  comes from Weight's missing landmarks, a topic's subject or 0.14's single scale, as follows. What the labels cannot
  settle is the Area must-read volume (below).
  - **Central × solid → must read** is 0.14's tier 2, not its tier 1: 137 of 159 tiered verdicts in Alignment and
    safety, 65 of 73 in Interpretability, 280 of 323 in Agents. 0.14 gave tier 1 to 11 papers in the window on its
    five broad selections, all of them Field topics. So v1's must read holds the papers 0.14 called relevant, one
    grade name higher. Moving the cell to relevant would leave must read to landmark Weight alone, which the run
    gives two papers in 6,294, neither in an Area: with Weight as it is, Area picks would have no candidates. Whether
    the cell should move once landmark is given at its intended rate is open (the Area must-read finding).
  - **Central × incremental → relevant** leans to 0.14's tier 3: Agents 27 to 14, Efficiency 29 to 8; Alignment is
    even at 13 to 11, and Interpretability goes the other way (0 to 6). The examples are small steps squarely on the
    subject (2610.04140, a per-expert Muon step size for MoE training; 2610.07032, distillation and quantization for
    one biomedical translation pair). 0.14 lowered small work a tier because its scale has no second axis; v1 keeps a
    central paper at relevant by design. Not changed.
  - **Related × solid → relevant** leans to 0.14's tier 3 in Interpretability (38 to 6) and in Alignment and safety
    (51 to 34). The examples split. Some use interpretability's methods as a tool for another question: 2610.04998
    steers activations against over-refusal, 2610.10655 steers for unlearning. The Fit definitions call that
    adjacent. Others are fair related papers: 2610.10276, a benchmark of activation patching's side effects;
    2610.01864, SAE alignment on music models. This is the Area judge's reading of related on topics where
    steering is a class of the subject, so it is a Fit and subject finding, not a cell to move.
  - **Central × solid at 0.14's tier 3 in Alignment and safety** (22) are mostly evaluation methodology:
    2610.04429, a scorer flaw in BFCL multi-turn; 2610.08026, labelling in hallucination benchmarks. v1's subject
    includes that strand and 0.14's did not.
  - **Related × incremental on a Focus topic → adjacent** gives no library save and an uncommon colour. Of its 14
    verdicts, the 4 that 0.14 screened include 2 of its tier 1–2 papers: 2610.07186 ("Identifying Introspection
    From the Inside", tier 1 on faithfulness) and 2610.00961 (monitoring), both provisional positives. Two papers
    cannot carry a change. Whether a related Focus paper should always be saved is a question for the owner, and
    the Focus digest lists both by Fit either way.
- **On the provisional positives, v1 is never more generous than the first v1.** Of the 16 pairs, it gives the same
  grade on 10 and a lower one on 6. Two of those 6 are related × incremental (above), three are related × solid
  where the first v1 had must read, and one is graded adjacent.
- **Weight almost never says landmark.** Landmark is 2 of 6,294 (0.03 %), against about one or two in a hundred;
  solid is 76 %, incremental 24 %.
  - Weight carries some signal: 93 % solid at 0.14's tier 2 against 66 % at tier 3.
  - Whether solid is given too often, these labels cannot say. It is 73 % even of 0.14's tier-4 papers, but 0.14's
    tier measures closeness to a selection, not importance in a paper's own area; and a reason like "a fair but
    narrow evaluation" (2610.05119) goes with a new method compared on three benchmarks, which solid allows.
  - None of 0.14's 20 tier-1 papers is landmark. Among them, 2610.02320 (DeskForge) releases code, data and weights,
    with gains across four models; 2610.02835 gains up to 13.4 points across benchmarks and model families.
  - What follows from it:
    - the landmark column applies to three verdicts on two papers, so must read is nearly all central × solid;
    - legendary appears once on a topic page (2610.17590 on Language models) and never in a mixed list;
    - uncommon, the solid floor, is the colour of 53 % of the papers.

  The missing landmarks are an analysis finding: an `analyse` prompt or model change, measured against Weight
  labels from the owner, or at least against 0.14's tiers. No milestone owns that yet.
- **Area must reads are 6–20 times the picks they feed.** Alignment and safety has a median of 20 must reads a night
  Tuesday to Friday (8–34; the partial 10-14 left out) and 63 and 35 on the Mondays; Interpretability has 12
  (4–14), with 25 and 14 on the Mondays. That is 37 % and 25 % of what each grades, while a daily digest picks 1–2.
  An Area's subject is broad, so central is common (43 % of Alignment and safety's verdicts), and with Weight as it
  is, must read is most of what is central. The labels cannot say where the excess comes from: Fit, Weight (whether
  solid is too common), the central × solid → must read cell, or a mix. Owner labels of Weight or grade on Area
  papers, and the results of N3's re-check, would tell them apart. Every must read waits for a full-text Weight
  re-check before it is a pick candidate, and that re-check is where the volume shows up next.
- **The full-text queue would be far larger than section 11 estimates.** The run's grades queue passes for 389
  distinct papers over the ten nights: 17–51 a night Tuesday to Friday, 51 and 92 on the Mondays, about 62 per 1,000
  papers. 353 of them are Area Weight re-checks; 92 qualify for a Focus Brief, and 56 papers are on both lists. N3's
  branch measures about $0.15 a pass on Opus 5.5, $0.30 for a text at the cut. At that price the passes would cost
  about $9 per 1,000 papers: more than three times everything this run spent per paper, against section 11's "cents to
  a dollar". This is the strongest reason to settle the Area must-read volume before the restart (N6).
- **Rarity is not scarce in a mixed list, and Field must reads are why.** A median night shows 29 % of its papers at
  rare or above (24–37 %), and epic at 3–8 %. Field must reads give 1,267 of the 1,834 papers at rare or above.
  - **Field offset 2** (Field must read → uncommon) brings rare or above to 9 % a night (6–12 %). Field grades would
    then never lift a paper above its Weight's floor in a mixed list, not even a must read with landmark (rare,
    which landmark's floor already gives): in the mixed list a Field topic would show as a badge, not a colour.
    Which badges a paper shows, their topic pages and the weekly picks are unchanged; the badges' order can change,
    since it follows the same offsets (a Language models badge can move behind a Focus topic's). **This is
    recommended, and it is the owner's decision**: an offset change is material, so it is not made here.
  - **Area offset 1** turns epic into rare (epic falls to 0.4 %: 24 Focus must reads and one Field landmark) and
    leaves rare or above at 26 %. It is not recommended now. Which Area must reads survive the full-text re-check is
    unknown until N3 runs.
  - Focus stays at 0. Its must reads are 0–3 a night per topic.
- **Recall insurance is most of Focus grading's cost, and it pays.** Focus gradings come to 145 per 1,000 papers;
  section 11 estimated 20–40 a night. The classifier brings in 13 per 1,000, the uncertain band 96 and the keyword
  check 36. The band and keyword gradings cost about $0.9 per 1,000 papers and bring 41 of the 99 related-or-above
  Focus gradings (band 34, keyword 7). The replay found the band carrying recall; here the keyword check also
  brings 7 papers the band did not.
- **Gist validation failed 174 of 6,468 analyse calls (2.7 %), all on a Gist too long.** 130 papers failed at least
  once and 11 failed three runs, which left them with no analysis.
  - The failed replies were not kept. So the 130 papers were asked again (11 of them three times), with the Gist
    guard lifted and the same request schema, from a script outside the repository: 152 answers, 21–42 words, none
    under 15. 30 % of them were over 35.
  - Even accepted Gists overshoot the target: 653 of 6,294 (10 %) are 31–35 words.
  - The 11 papers' Gists were long because the papers pack several results ("… sublinear regret. It reaches higher
    accuracy at lower verification cost …", 38 words). They were not broken.
  - The guard (`GIST_WORDS` in `analysis/__init__.py`) now accepts 15–45 words: one and a half times the 30 asked
    for, against a broken answer, and over the 42 seen. The prompt still asks for 20–30.
  - Run again on a copy of `n2c_run` with the owner's "try again", all 11 were analysed. 6 of them came out at 36–42
    words, which the old guard would have failed again.
  - Bringing Gists back inside 30 is a prompt question for the quality harness, not the guard.
- **One paper is ungraded, by refusal.** Opus 5.5 refused three times, as `refused: bio`, to grade 2610.13116
  ("DRYLAB-Bench: Benchmarking the Biosecurity Risk of Agentic LLM Mutation Design") for Alignment and safety. It
  is listed on Quality as designed. Grading it now would ask the same model again, so on a refusal only the owner's
  word can grade it. Asking `check`, the other lab, is a design question left open.

## Volume

Section 11's "700–1,550 papers on a weekday, about 2,500 on the first day after a weekend" counts something else. It
is the first v1's nightly intake from 2026-09-29 to 2026-10-12, which comes by announcement through OAI-PMH. This run
reads the archive's papers by their first version's submission date: 425–595 on a weekday (424 on the partial last
day), 975 and 1,156 on a night that folds in Saturday and Sunday. Two differences account for the gap, and both were
checked against the first v1's database (its dump of 2026-10-14, restored to a scratch database and dropped
afterwards):

- **The first v1's nightly records include later versions.** Of the 10,991 papers it fetched in that window, 3,454
  (31 %) carried a version after the first (its `version` column, read at each paper's last fetch, so a paper
  fetched new and again as revised counts once, as revised). v1 processes a revision again only when its title or
  abstract is rewritten (section 8); how many of these were, and what they would cost, was not measured.
- **That window began in the busiest week.** The week of 2026-09-28 had 4,177 submissions in the first v1's database;
  the week after had 3,134 in the archive.

The archive is not missing papers the first v1 had. For 2026-10-01 to 10-08, where the first v1's intake was
complete, all 3,877 of its papers are in the run, which holds 15 more: papers numbered late (2610.14091–14101,
2610.18877–18890) and announced after the first v1's dump was taken. 2026-10-14 is partial:
papers submitted after that day's cutoff were announced after the run started.

## Cost per weekday

Per 1,000 papers, from the call journal: classify $0.26, analyse $0.40 (failed calls $0.03 of the run's $2.50),
judge $1.79, check $0.07, area_check $0.01, field_grade $0.16; $2.68 in all. By depth:

- Focus grading is $1.00 per 1,000 (judge $5.83 and check $0.46 over 915 gradings).
- Area grading is $0.87 per 1,000 (judge $5.43, area_check $0.04 and check $0.01 over 1,128 gradings; 14 escalated
  to `check`).
- Field grading is $0.16 per 1,000 (5,111 gradings).

The judge's calls were matched to the verdicts they made by their reason. The run processed the fortnight at once;
its costs split by submission day, weekends folded into Monday, give $1.04–1.70 for a night Tuesday to Friday
(424–595 papers) and $2.45 and $3.29 for the two Mondays (975 and 1,156). Anthropic carried $14.76, OpenRouter $2.12.
Section 11 now carries these numbers beside its estimates.
