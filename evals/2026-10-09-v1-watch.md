# v1 Watch pipeline: first trial (milestone 2)

2026-10-09, branch `v1`, on a copy of the production database (23,468 papers). All runs are
dry runs (`litwatch eval …`): they write to `eval_runs` and `eval_results` only.

## What was tested

Whether v1's pipeline (one triage call per paper, a keyword audit, then two graders from
different labs) puts forward and keeps the papers 0.14 placed at tier 1 or 2 on the two CoT
topics, and what it costs. Topics are the production selections as of today, imported
unchanged; the grading prompt explains that the rubrics' tiers map to grades.

This does **not** measure what v1 misses that 0.14 also missed. Only the reject sample below
can show that, and it is not labelled yet.

The provisional positives are model output. Agreement with them shows v1 has not regressed
from 0.14; it is not recall.

## Triage (Haiku 5.5, medium effort)

Share of 0.14 tier 1–2 papers that the triage model alone passed, before the keyword audit:

| Prompt | cot-faithfulness (39) | cot-monitoring (51) | Lost after the audit |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| v1: yes or no, "could plausibly belong" | 36 | 44 | 6 |
| v2: same, told it is a coarse filter and exclusions are for the judge | 36 | 46 | 5 |
| v3: about / related / unrelated, only unrelated stops a paper | 39 | 50 | 1 |

Asked yes or no, Haiku judged membership: its reasons read "it does not test whether the
trace matches the computation", which is the grader's job. Rewording did not change that, and
the papers lost differed between v1 and v2, so part of it is noise at the boundary. A middle
answer fixed it. What is stored is still a yes or a no.

The one paper lost under v3 is 2609.15517 ("Beyond Safe Answers: Segment-Aware Listwise
Alignment for Reasoning Safety", 0.14 tier 2 on monitoring); both graders call it adjacent
when forced. On the 8 gold positives triage passed 7; the keyword audit caught the eighth.

Cost: $0.00028 a call on average (about 2,800 input tokens, most read from cache), so about
$0.18 for a night of 650 papers. 9 of about 5,700 calls timed out.

## Grading (Opus 5.5 medium as judge)

Every labelled paper was graded for both topics, whether or not it became a candidate.

| | Muse Spark 1.3 contributor, high | Luna (gpt-6-luna), medium |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Provisional positives graded relevant or above, faithfulness (39) | 37 | 37 |
| Same, monitoring (51) | 50 | 50 |
| Gold positives graded relevant or above (8) | 5 | 5 |
| Split verdicts of 184 | 45 | 59 |
| Mean / 90th percentile / longest call, seconds | 14.6 / 24.1 / 59.3 | 11.9 / 19.4 / 36.1 |
| Failed calls of 184 | 0 | 0 |
| Cost per call | $0.00054 | $0.00013 |

The reconciled grade is the same under either check model for 170 of 184 pairs. Opus as
judge took 2.7 s and $0.0050 a call.

The three gold positives graded adjacent (2502.05242, 2507.12379, 2512.01222) carry labels
"converted from v1 must_read … unverified". Both graders give the same reason each time: the
paper reads internals or improves monitorability without testing whether a trace matches the
computation.

Andrei re-read the three after this run and agreed with the graders, so the labels were wrong,
not the rubric. All three are now labelled adjacent for cot-faithfulness. Two belong to
cot-monitoring instead and are labelled there: 2512.01222 as must-read (both graders:
must-read) and 2502.05242 as relevant (judge adjacent, check relevant, so relevant with a
split). With the corrected labels the graders match every gold positive: 5 of 5 on
cot-faithfulness and 2 of 2 on cot-monitoring. The table above is the run as it was scored,
before the correction.

Of the 2 gold negatives, one ("Faithful Chain-of-Thought Reasoning", 2301.13379) is graded
relevant.

## Expected spend

About $0.50 a night at 650 papers: triage $0.18, judge $0.29, check $0.03. Real dollars are
the check model alone, about $1 a month; the rest is Anthropic credit. The spec estimated a
few cents for grading. The difference is the keyword audit's volume.

## Provisional choices

- **Triage:** Haiku 5.5 at medium effort with the three-way answer.
- **Judge:** Opus 5.5 at medium effort.
- **Check:** Muse Spark contributor at high effort. It agrees with the judge more often than
  Luna, which means fewer splits to settle, at no difference in what is kept. Its slowest
  call here took 59 s, inside the 180 s timeout.

These are to be confirmed against Inbox labels, as the spec says.

## The reject sample, labelled

The owner labelled all 100 drawn rejects for both topics on 2026-10-09 (eval run 5 for the
draw, run 7 for the replay with forced grading).

| Stratum | Rejects in the pool | Drawn | Relevant | Unsure (papers) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| In a Radar field (near) | 262 | 70 | 0 | 3 |
| Everything else (far) | 2,614 | 30 | 0 | 2 |

The one paper first labelled relevant, 2609.00067 ("Do Multimodal LLMs See Before They Read?
Diagnosing Contextual Sycophancy", cot-faithfulness), the owner reclassified as adjacent: it
does not evaluate faithfulness under the topic's definition. Both graders call it `none`
when forced. So the sample contains no paper the owner calls relevant: 0 of 70 and 0 of 30.

What the sample supports: among rejects in the Radar fields the miss rate is below about 4%
(0 of 70, 95% upper bound). For the far stratum, 0 of 30 only bounds the rate below about
10%, which over 2,614 papers is no bound worth quoting. A
useful bound there needs a few hundred labels, or a smarter draw.

Forced grading of the rejects agrees with the labels: of 191 pairs labelled not relevant, 186
are graded `none` and 5 `adjacent`; none reaches `relevant`.

The owner used unsure for "interesting, an idea supplement, not directly relevant". Three of
the five such papers (AYLA, SAE++, a random-forest interpretability framework) have nothing
to do with reasoning traces. That is two different things: `adjacent` to a topic, and
interesting regardless of topic. Neither is uncertainty about relevance. Labels now have a
fourth value, adjacent, which is not a positive for recall.
